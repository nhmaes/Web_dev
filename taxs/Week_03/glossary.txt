Open source:
Software whose source code is available for people to inspect, use, and often modify or share under a license.
Closed source: 
Software whose source code is kept private and can only be used under the owner's terms.
Trojan horse: 
Malicious software disguised as a harmless or useful program, which can carry out unwanted actions once installed.
Cyberspace: 
The interconnected digital world of the internet, computers, and online communication.
Cyberwarfare: 
The use of cyberattacks by or on behalf of a country to disrupt, damage, or spy on another country's systems.
Cyberfeminism: 
The study and use of digital technology to explore or challenge gender inequality and advance feminist ideas.
Fibre: 
A thin strand of material; in technology, fibre-optic cable carries data as pulses of light.
Censorship: 
The control or suppression of information, speech, or media that an authority considers objectionable.
Emerging tech: 
New or rapidly developing technology that may significantly affect how people live or work.
Malware: 
Software designed to harm, disrupt, spy on, or gain unauthorized access to a device or computer system.
The Cloud: 
A way of storing data and using software or computing power on remote internet-connected servers instead of only on a personal device.
Cookies: 
Small pieces of data that a website stores in your browser to remember information, such as your preferences or whether you are signed in.
